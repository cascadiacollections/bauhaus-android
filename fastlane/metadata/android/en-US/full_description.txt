Bauhaus puts a fresh piece of art on your home screen and lock screen every day.

Each morning a public-domain landscape from the Metropolitan Museum of Art or the Art Institute of Chicago is run through neural style transfer against a curated reference — Monet, Hokusai, Cezanne, Turner, Hiroshige, Seurat, Degas, Klimt, Van Gogh, Gauguin — and published. The app fetches it and sets it as your wallpaper.

Features:
• A new stylized artwork every day, applied automatically in the background
• Set it on your home screen, lock screen, or both
• Browse and re-apply any day from the archive
• Save the full-resolution image to your gallery, or share it
• Favorite the days you want to keep
• Title, artist, source collection and licence shown for every piece, with links

Privacy:
• No account, no sign-in, no ads
• No analytics, tracking, or crash reporting in this build
• Two permissions only: INTERNET, to fetch the artwork, and SET_WALLPAPER
• Nothing is collected or shared. The app sends no identifiers — it makes plain
  GET requests to the artwork service and nothing else.

Licensing and attribution:
• Source artwork is CC0 (public domain) from the Met and Art Institute of Chicago
• The style-transfer model is AdaIN (MIT); the VGG-19 encoder is BSD-like
• Generated artwork from the daily scheduled runs is CC0-1.0
• Each image's licence and upstream source are shown in the app and published
  alongside it in the artwork metadata
